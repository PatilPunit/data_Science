Data visualization is the process of transforming raw data into graphical representations, such as charts, graphs, and plots, to facilitate the interpretation of patterns, trends, and anomalies.  By converting complex numerical datasets into visual formats, it enables decision-makers to quickly understand insights and make informed choices more effectively than analyzing raw tables. 

A bar graph (or bar chart) is a specific visualization tool that represents categorical or discrete data using rectangular bars.  The length or height of each bar is proportional to the value or frequency it represents, allowing for easy comparison between different groups. Key characteristics include:

Purpose: Primarily used to compare values across distinct categories, such as sales by region or votes for candidates. 

Structure: One axis displays the categories, while the other shows the numerical values; bars are separated by gaps to emphasize that the data is distinct. 

Variations: Includes vertical (column) charts, horizontal charts, grouped charts, and stacked charts to handle multiple data points or hierarchical data. 

Distinction: Unlike histograms, which show the distribution of continuous data with touching bars, bar graphs are designed for separate, non-continuous categories.

A frequency table is a statistical tool that organizes data by listing values or categories alongside the number of times each occurs, known as the frequency.  It summarizes the distribution of a dataset, making it easier to identify patterns, trends, and the most common or rare values.
