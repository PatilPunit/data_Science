A density curve is a smoothed, idealized graph that represents the probability distribution of a continuous variable. Unlike a histogram, it shows the relative frequency of outcomes over an infinite number of intervals, where the total area underneath the curve is exactly 1 (or 100%).

Mean vs. Median

The shape of the curve dictates where the measures of center lie:

Symmetric Curves (e.g., Normal Distribution): The mean and the median are identical and sit exactly in the center of the distribution.

Skewed Curves: The median divides the area under the curve exactly in half (50% above, 50% below). The mean is pulled toward the "tail" of the skew (e.g., if the tail stretches to the right, the mean will be greater than the median).

Estimating Standard Deviation (Normal Curve)For a classic bell-shaped (normal) density curve, the standard deviation is the measure of the curve's spread:

Empirical Rule: Approximately 68% of the data falls within \(1\) standard deviation of the mean, 95% falls within \(2\) standard deviations, and 99.7% within \(3\) standard deviations.
