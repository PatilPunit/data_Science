Measures of spread (also called measures of dispersion) are statistical metrics that describe how varied, scattered, or spread out a set of data values are.  They complement measures of central tendency (like the mean or median) by indicating how representative the center is of the entire dataset; a small spread implies data points are clustered closely around the center, while a large spread indicates significant variability. 

Range is the simplest and most basic measure of spread, defined specifically as the difference between the maximum and minimum values in a dataset ( Range=Maximum−Minimum ). While the range provides a quick estimate of the total span of the data, it is highly sensitive to outliers and extreme values because it relies solely on the two most distant points, often failing to accurately represent the spread of the majority of the data. Other measures, such as the interquartile range (IQR), variance, and standard deviation, are often preferred for more robust analysis as they account for the distribution of all data points and are less affected by extreme outliers
